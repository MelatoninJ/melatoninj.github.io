About

Minjae Kim is a senior at Seoul Science High School, drawn to synthetic biology and working toward becoming a biological and biomedical engineer. His interests are morphogenesis, spatial omics, programmable organoids and cell–cell communication.

He designs molecules with bioinformatics and AI, and at the same time works out where those models break down in the setting he is designing for. Through the invention club he is equally at home building the hardware an experiment needs and writing the software that runs it — Arduino, CAD, Raspberry Pi, Programming.

A question that began in a biomedical engineering R&E now runs through his graduate thesis and past it: using RFdiffusion, AlphaFold and Boltz-2 he designs and expresses VHHs against a membrane protein, and tests whether they can capture cells selectively.

Design and Expression of an RFdiffusion-based Anti-EpCAM VHH

A molecular glue that holds a cell without disturbing its state.

Graduate thesis2026Computational + wet lab
Built
Designed with RFdiffusion, ProteinMPNN and RoseTTAFold2. In the course of designing VHHs a flaw in AlphaFold2 surfaced, so the designs were re-evaluated with Boltz-2, which proved the more trustworthy of the two — and all 2,960 were screened again before one was carried forward.
For
Patterning a scaffold so a chosen cell lands in a chosen place — setting the initial condition, location, from which cell–cell communication and junction formation can be guided.
Now
Cloned into pRSET-C, expressed in BL21(DE3), purified over Ni-NTA, confirmed by anti-His6 Western at ~16.4 kDa. Binding assays under way.
Role
Carried out alone. Dry lab: epitope selection, the design pipeline, the artifact analysis and predictor benchmark, and filtering all 2,960 designs. Wet lab: everything from cloning through purification.
02

Functional Decoupling

Two routes to loss of function.

Independent research2026Variant effect prediction
Built
A protein has two gates to clear before it works in a cell: it has to fold and stay folded, and the chemistry of its reactive domain has to be intact. I split failures of function by which gate was missed — too little protein in the cell, or enough protein that no longer works — and asked how the two divide. Working from four proteins whose abundance and activity were both measured, I found that which of the two routes a variant takes is itself predictable (AUC 0.80–0.92), and that the prediction still holds on a protein left out of training.
For
When a patient's variant is interpreted, clinical genetics places it on a single scale from pathogenic to benign. But the two kinds of breakdown separated here call for different treatment: a folding-stability problem can be approached with a chaperone, while a variant that is present in normal amounts and simply inactive cannot.
Now
Analysis complete on the four datasets; looking for more.
Role
Carried out alone, on the school's server.
03

Causal Analysis of Fault Propagation under Electromagnetic Fault Injection Attacks

A knockout screen run on a microcontroller.

GTC-ISPC2025Hardware + causal analysis
Built
Treated fault injection as a knockout screen: chemically decapsulated an ATmega2560, scanned the exposed die with 50 ns electromagnetic pulses short enough to land inside one clock cycle, and read the registers, program output and cycle count as the phenotype. A causal model built on the AVR block diagram recovered two dominant propagation paths — one corrupting control flow, one corrupting data.
For
Designing microcontrollers that fail predictably under fault injection, instead of cataloguing faults after the fact.
Now
Presented at GTC-ISPC 2025, Physics & Engineering. Second runner-up.
Role
Proposed the knockout framing; led the causal analysis and the presentation.
04

Romeo and Juliet with Morse Code

Letters between two schools, sent in laser morse.

Invention club2024Optical comms
Built
A line-of-sight optical link to the roof of the school next door: a laser on a tripod at one end, a photoresistor and an Arduino inside a wooden box at the other. The receiver measures its own threshold against the ambient light at start-up — one reading with the beam blocked, one with it on the sensor, and the boundary halfway between — then recovers dot, dash and the breaks between letters and words from nothing but how long the beam stays on and off. The link carried over 300 m.
For
Talking across a gap with no network and no radio — and for the romance of it.
Now
Working. The first sentence through the beam asked for the Instagram ID of a cute boy!
05

GelCutGuide

Borrowed from a japanese noodle master's cutting board.

Invention club2026Lab tooling
Built
A 3D-printed jig for excising DNA bands from agarose, modelled in CAD as three frames sized to the gel trays in our lab. Version 1 marked where to cut, but the frame itself got in the way — the razor blade could not reach the square it was pointing at. The fix came from a hand-made udon board, where the blade is carried by the jig rather than by the hand: version 2 adds a hinge and a blade reference, so the blade swings cleanly into the cut and stays in its lane.
For
Cutting the right band when the transilluminator strips away depth and outline behind the goggles.
Now
v2 revised, and the electrophoresis test is done. The test showed the next problem: the bar covers the band at the moment you are lining up on it. The next version has that piece laser-cut from clear acrylic — the line is still set by the bar, but you can see the band through it.
06

Maker Festival

1v1 RC Car Obstacle Racing Game

Invention club2024Public exhibit
Built
A 1v1 game on an RC car course, borrowed from Fall Guys — one player drives, the other does everything to stall them. Hand-drawn layout, redrawn in CAD, cut, then wired: the blockers are servo-driven barriers that slide into the lane on a button press. Each one carries a length of rack meshed with a gear on the servo, so a rotation a servo can actually manage becomes straight travel — and the second player is pressing buttons against a car instead of just watching.
For
An interesting encounter with invention, for primary and middle school students.
Now
Ran as a hands-on booth for over 100 primary and middle school teams at the 2024 Science–Math Convergence Maker Festival, start to finish.

Software

p2d
v0.2 PYTHON | CLI | LOCAL UI MIT
Try it in the browser

A tool that designs the DNA for the protein you want to insert — codon optimisation included — against the vector and the restriction enzymes you are actually using. p2d reverse-translates into DNA that can really be cloned into the plasmid you give it, and shows the fusion protein that will be expressed, scar residues and all. It began as something built for his own cloning and was opened up once it was doing the job.

$ git clone https://github.com/MelatoninJ/protein2dna && cd protein2dna && pip install -e .

p2d ui opens a local website in the browser, p2d design and p2d batch run from the terminal, and import p2d scripts it. The site is an easier way to use p2d for anyone who isn't used to python.

Reads your plasmid from GenBank, SnapGene or FASTA — and finds the start codon from the sequence itself when the file carries no annotation
Suggests every usable pair of restriction sites, grouped by the protein each pair would produce
N- and C-terminal tags and linkers, or a read-through into the vector's own tag with the frame bases added
Codons provably free of both enzymes' sites — on either strand, and across the junctions with the vector
Round-trip check: the order is cut and ligated in silico and must rebuild the planned plasmid exactly
Batch design from a FASTA; exports CSV, FASTA and an annotated GenBank per construct

Currently developing the quality by gathering more data.
